# TRAVEL LIFE — MASTER PRODUCT CONTEXT

## 1. Product Thesis

### Vision

Build the **digital home for a person's entire travel life**.

Travel Life automatically reconstructs where someone has travelled from data they already possess, builds a persistent **Personal Travel Graph**, and eventually uses that history to understand the traveller and help them make better future travel decisions.

### Consumer promise

> **See your entire travel life.**

### Core experience

> Connect your photos once → Travel Life reconstructs your journeys → explore your lifetime map/timeline → revisit memories → share your Travel Life → it continues growing automatically as you travel.

### Long-term category thesis

**Personal Travel Intelligence**

Internally this describes the strategic direction.

It should not necessarily be the initial consumer-facing positioning.

---

# 2. What We Are NOT Building

Travel Life is not primarily:

- photo storage
- photo cleaner
- photo organizer
- photo editor
- travel journal
- trip tracker
- trip planner
- booking marketplace
- generic travel social network
- AI chatbot

Google Photos/Apple Photos already have powerful photo organization, memories, AI search, highlights and reels.

Therefore:

> **Photo intelligence is infrastructure/input, not the moat.**

Our core object is the **journey/travel life**, not the photo.

---

# 3. Core Strategic Insight

People accumulate enormous amounts of digital evidence about their lives without accumulating a structured representation of those lives.

Travel evidence is fragmented across:

- phone photos
- Google Photos
- videos
- location metadata
- calendars
- flights
- hotels
- emails
- maps
- social media
- computers/external drives
- memory

Travel Life turns fragmented evidence into:

**Raw Data → Presence → Trips → Destinations → Personal Travel Graph → Travel Identity → Personal Travel Intelligence**

The long-term asset is not the AI model.

It is a continuously improving representation of the user's travel history.

```text
PERSON
 │
 ├── Years
 │    └── Travel Episodes
 │          ├── Countries
 │          ├── Cities
 │          ├── Destinations
 │          ├── Dates
 │          ├── Routes
 │          ├── Travel Days
 │          └── Memories
 │
 ├── Patterns
 ├── Repeat Destinations
 ├── Seasons
 ├── Travel Frequency
 ├── Preferences
 └── Behaviour
```

Over time this can answer:

**Record**
- Where have I been?
- When?
- How often?
- For how long?

**Identity**
- What kind of traveller am I?
- Where do I repeatedly return?
- How has my travel life changed?

**Intelligence**
- Where should I go next?
- What is relevant to me now?
- Which destinations fit my actual behaviour?

# 11. Travel Presence Inference

A major insight from analysis:

> A photo being in someone's library does NOT prove the person visited that location.

Examples:

- friend sends Japan photos
- downloaded Instagram image
- WhatsApp image
- screenshot
- edited/exported media
- migrated library
- incorrect metadata

Therefore the intelligence pipeline should be:

```text
Media
 ↓
Presence Evidence
 ↓
Location Confidence
 ↓
Travel Episode Clustering
 ↓
Destination Resolution
 ↓
Personal Travel Graph
```

Before asking:

> “Was this one trip?”

we should ask:

> **“Do we have sufficient evidence that this user was actually there?”**

Potential evidence:

- original photo metadata
- timestamps
- GPS
- sequential geographic movement
- photo density
- multiple-day continuity
- device-origin signals
- future location/calendar/booking evidence

This should become an important technical capability and possible moat.

---

# 12. Travel Episode Detection

Do not require users to manually create trips.

State concept:

```text
NORMAL LIFE
   ↓
POTENTIAL TRAVEL EPISODE
   ↓
ACTIVE TRAVEL EPISODE
   ↓
COMPLETED
   ↓
NEW TRAVEL CHAPTER
```

Potential signals:

- new country
- new city
- distance from usual locations
- multi-day continuity
- overnight pattern
- photo/video density
- geographic movement
- multiple destinations
- return to stable routine

Use confidence rather than hard rules.

Illustrative only:

```text
New country           +40
New city              +20
Distance              +20
Multiple days         +30
Overnight activity    +30
Photo density         +20
Route continuity      +20
```

Production weights/model must come from testing.

---

# 13. Human-in-the-Loop

AI should automate the common case and ask users about exceptions.

Example:

> 🇯🇵 We think this was a Japan trip  
> Oct 1–12  
> Tokyo · Kyoto · Osaka  
> 846 memories

Actions:

**Add | Edit | Not a Trip**

Principle:

> **AI does the work. Humans correct exceptions.**

Corrections should eventually improve future inference.

```text

Takes photos normally

 ↓

 ↓
```

# 23. Privacy & Trust

This is both a product requirement AND GTM problem.

We potentially ask users for access to:

- years of photos
- locations
- family memories
- relationships
- homes
- documents
- private moments

Actual onboarding funnel:

```text
PROMISE
 ↓
TRUST
 ↓
PERMISSION
 ↓
PROCESSING
 ↓
MAGIC
```

If Trust → Permission fails, the product fails.

Principles:

1. Explain value before permission.
2. Request only necessary access.
3. Allow source-level control.
4. Allow disconnect.
5. Allow deletion.
6. Clearly explain processing.
7. Minimize original-media retention.
8. Prefer local/on-device processing where feasible.
9. Do not sell personal travel-history data.
10. Make privacy architecture part of positioning.

# 24. Retention Problem

Automatic Travel Episode Detection reduces maintenance friction.

It does **not necessarily create frequent engagement**.

If someone travels 3 times/year, automatic trip updates may only produce three major reactivation moments.

Therefore current Travel Life may be:

> WOW → Explore → Share → Leave → Return after trip

That can still be useful, but long-term retention remains an open problem.

Potential solutions:

- annual Travel Wrapped
- memories
- historical insights
- Travel DNA
- meaningful new patterns
- Personal Travel Intelligence
- relevant Travel Pulse information
- destination discovery
- future-trip recommendations

These should be tested rather than automatically built.

# 29. Key Product Risks

### Platform risk

Photo APIs/permissions may prevent reliable access.

### Data-quality risk

Historical libraries are messy.

### Trust risk

Users refuse broad access.

### Accuracy risk

Incorrect travel history damages trust quickly.

### Novelty risk

Users love first reveal but never return.

### Competition risk

Google/Apple could replicate visible features.

### Cost risk

Multimodal processing at scale could become expensive.

### Viral risk

Users may consider travel history too private to share.

### Monetization risk

Emotional product ≠ willingness to subscribe.

---

# 30. Product Principles

1. **Zero maintenance**
   - user should not manually journal trips.

2. **AI first, correction second**
   - automate, then ask about uncertainty.

3. **Travel Graph > photos**
   - photos are evidence.

4. **Identity before gamification**
   - show meaningful facts, not meaningless levels.

5. **Privacy is product**
   - architecture should reinforce trust.

6. **Incremental intelligence**
   - start simple; improve with evidence.

7. **Don't build future vision before validating current behaviour.**

8. **The shared artifact should be about the user, not Travel Life.**

9. **Build around emotional moments, not feature count.**

10. **Each phase must earn the right to build the next.**
